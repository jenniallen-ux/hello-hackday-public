    Watch-outs from the research

      Field guide

      The short version of the science behind the questions. Neither mode is better in general. Each one moves different kinds of work off the person's mind and onto the system.

      Conversation carries well

      Compound intent
      One sentence can hold several constraints that a UI would spread across screens and filters.
      Fuzzy needs
      Clarifying questions narrow a problem the way a good advisor does. Forms assume people know which fields matter.
      Rare tasks
      No layout to learn. People describe the goal and skip the learning curve.
      Explanation, practice and reflection
      Self-explanation, retrieval practice and tutoring dialogue all strengthen learning, and all are conversational by nature.
      Eyes-busy, hands-busy moments
      Audio uses a different attention channel from the visual-manual task in front of someone.

      A surface carries well

      More than about four things at once
      A screen acts as external memory. Speech vanishes as it's heard.
      Comparison
      Eyes jump between table cells in parallel. Conversation delivers attributes one at a time.
      Spatial, visual and structural content
      Maps, layouts, images and diagrams show relationships that words have to serialise.
      Precise adjustment
      Direct manipulation gives continuous, reversible feedback in a single gesture.
      Monitoring and review
      Glanceable state shows change without asking, and visible details are easier to check before an irreversible step.
      Habit
      Frequent tasks get faster on a stable layout that people learn by position.
